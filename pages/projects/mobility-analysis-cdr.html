Mobility Analysis from Call Detail Records

Call detail records · 1.5M users

Project overview

During the COVID-19 lockdowns in 2020, governments used mobile network data to check whether movement restrictions were holding. Call Detail Records (CDRs) make that possible: every connection to a cell tower leaves a trace that estimates where a user is over time. This project builds the processing side of that idea, a PySpark pipeline that turns raw CDRs for 1.5 million users into per-user mobility metrics.

PythonPySparkPandas UDFsNumPySeabornMatplotlib

Objectives

Process call records for 1.5 million users with PySpark, generate summary statistics, and explore the relationship between the average number of unique locations per user per day and the average radius of gyration.

Data

Call records for 1.5 million users over 14 days, from 29 June to 13 July 2018. Each record is joined to cell-tower coordinates, and records missing a cell ID, latitude or longitude are dropped.

Methods and workflow

Preprocessing runs in Spark. The radius of gyration, the distance a user typically moves from their centre of activity, is computed with a pandas UDF, which is much faster than converting the Spark DataFrame to pandas first. Repartitioning, caching and sampling during debugging keep the pipeline scalable.

Findings

Activity per user is heavily skewed: the median user has 3 records, the mean is about 7.8, three quarters of users have 8 or fewer, and the most active user has 8,277. Average radius of gyration and average unique locations per day are positively but modestly correlated (r = 0.32).
